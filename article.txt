THE SÜMELA MONASTERY: A SPIRITUAL FORTRESS IN THE CLIFFS
1. Introduction and Geographical Significance
The Sümela Monastery, officially known as Panagia Soumela (the Virgin Mary of Soumela), is a Greek Orthodox monastery complex located in the Maçka district of Trabzon. It is built into a steep cliff on the slope of Karadağ (Black Mountain) within the Altındere Valley. The monastery stands about 300 meters above the valley floor and 1,150 meters above sea level.
Because it is built directly into the cliffs and surrounded by nature, it has a very unique and impressive appearance. For this reason, it was included in the UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List on February 25, 2000.

2. Historical Background and Legends
According to the Turkish Ministry of Culture and Tourism, the monastery was founded during the reign of Byzantine Emperor Theodosius I (375–395 AD) by two monks from Athens, Barnabas and Sophronios. It is said that both monks had the same dream in which the Virgin Mary showed them the location of a sacred icon. After this, they met in Trabzon and began building the monastery in a cave.
The monastery became more developed during the Empire of Trebizond. Alexios III (1349–1390) played an important role in its expansion and is often considered the main founder of the current structure. His importance can still be seen in the frescoes inside the monastery.
After the Ottoman conquest of Trabzon in 1461, the monastery continued to be protected. Ottoman sultans such as Fatih Sultan Mehmet and Yavuz Sultan Selim supported it and allowed it to continue its activities. This shows that the monastery had importance for different cultures and religions.

3. Architectural and Artistic Features
The Sümela Monastery is not just a single building. It is a large complex that includes the Main Rock Church, chapels, kitchens, student rooms, a guest house, a library, and a holy spring.
The Rock Church is the most important part. It was created by shaping a natural cave, and its walls are covered with frescoes. These paintings show scenes from the Bible, as well as the life of Jesus Christ and the Virgin Mary.
Another interesting part is the hidden sections that were discovered during restoration, such as the “Chamber of Affliction” and the “Watchtower Chapel.”
The holy spring is also very important. Water flows from the rocks into a small pool, and people have believed for centuries that this water has healing properties. Both Christians and Muslims have visited this place for this reason.

4. Cultural Heritage and Restoration
The name “Sümela” is believed to come from the Greek word “melas,” meaning black, possibly referring to the dark color of the mountain.
After being abandoned in 1923, the monastery was damaged over time. However, a major restoration project started in 2015 and lasted about 3.5 years. During this process, thousands of tons of rocks were removed to make the area safer, and some hidden parts of the monastery were revealed.
Today, Sümela Monastery is an important cultural and historical site. In my opinion, it is one of the most impressive places in Turkey because of its location and history. It reflects both Byzantine and Ottoman influences and shows the multicultural structure of Anatolia.
